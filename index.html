Critical evaluation of our survey

What limits our conclusions

Sample size

40+ per version is good, but CIs stay wide. Effects like Q6 need a larger sample.

Sample make-up

Mostly students, not representative. Groups differ (WG/dorm 54 % vs 39 %).

Two changes at once

Q4 mixed a statistic with other wording – the cause of an effect is not 100 % clear.

Too manipulative

Should be more careful with the statements the next time – was too obvious.

Could be a better topic

Topic was maybe already too opinion-heavy: it is a law, and people are often for the law rather than against it.

Summary of findings

Design changes the outcome – but not every bias worked

1

Wording and option order work

Flipped statement (Q3 90 % vs 63 %) and option order (Q7 22:00 vs 23:00; Q9 250 € vs 50 €) shifted answers significantly.

2

Numbers that state the same fact don’t

“67 % disturbed” vs “33 % comfortable” gave identical estimates Q10.

3

The metric decides what you see

With outliers the mean hid the Q9 effect completely – the median showed it. Most other effects pointed the right way but need a larger sample.
